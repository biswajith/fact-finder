[p4.1] 1. Statement of Work
[p4.h1] 1.1. Purpose
[p4.2] The purpose of this request for proposal (RFP) is to invite qualified implementation partners to submit a proposal for the deployment and integration of Oracle's Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP) and Utility Billing solutions for the City of Durham in North Carolina (the City).
[p4.3] This RFP outlines the City’s specific requirements regarding the implementation, customization, and operational integration of the Oracle solution.
[p4.4] The City requires a partner with proven expertise in effectively implementing Oracle's solutions, particularly in areas critical to local government operations such as utility billing (including customer accounts management, meter reading, and customer billing processes).
[p4.5] Proponents should demonstrate a comprehensive approach to meet all the outlined requirements in this RFP, emphasizing their experience and success in similar projects.
[p4.6] This solicitation represents the second phase of a two-step process.
[p4.7] The first phase involved the selection of Oracle as the Enterprise software solution.
[p4.8] This RFP focuses exclusively on seeking a partner for the implementation phase.
[p4.9] It is important to note that any proponent involved in the initial software solution phase, including VARs / Resellers of Oracle licenses, will neither be given preferential treatment nor be excluded from this implementation RFP process.
[p4.10] The City is committed to a fair and transparent selection process, focusing on securing the most competent and compatible partner for this critical implementation phase.
[p4.11] The Operating Model, as depicted in the figure below, serves as a strategic framework guiding operational decisions.
[p4.12] It plays a crucial role in defining the boundaries and scope of the system, as well as determining which mega-processes (represented by the grey bars in the middle) will receive implementation support.
[p4.13] This model is instrumental in consolidating core operational processes into comprehensive mega-processes.
[p4.14] These conglomerates are essentially a series of interconnected, sequential processes that work synergistically to produce a unified output.
[p4.h2] 1.2. ERP Project Overview
[p4.15] The City, in collaboration with Info-Tech Research Group Inc (Info-Tech), is now advancing to the next critical phase of its enterprise solution journey – the implementation of the selected Oracle solution.
[p4.16] This process, integral to the successful deployment of the ERP and Utility Billing solutions, will be conducted through a structured and collaborative approach, adhering to the proven Info-Tech methodology for ERP Implementation projects.
[p5.1] The project continues to be strategically aligned with the City's business vision and goals, ensuring that the implementation phase is driven by the business requirements previously established.
[p5.2] The focus is now on partnering with a capable and experienced Oracle implementation partner, who will be instrumental in transforming the defined future business processes and IT requirements into a fully functional Oracle environment.
[p5.3] The City's Partner selection decision will be based on Partner’s competency evaluated across a number of factors.
[p5.4] Factors to be considered include State/City Implementation Experience, Oracle ERP Solution Implementation experience, Collaboration, Communication & Support, Implementation Methodology, Proponent viability, Proponent capability, customer references, willingness to work cooperatively, and overall price.
[p5.5] The City will be establishing a business-critical relationship with the Proponent whose implementation services are retained.
[p5.6] One of the most significant requirements of a Proponent is the Proponent’s willingness to work cooperatively with the City, respecting the City's culture and wishes.
[p5.7] The Proponent's willingness to adhere to the selection process set forth in this document will be viewed by the City as an indicator of the nature of the future relationship.

[p6.h2] 2.2. About City of Durham
[p6.3] Durham (/ˈdʌrəm/DURR-əm) is a city in the U.S. state of North Carolina and the county seat of Durham County.(7) Small portions of the city limits extend into Orange County and Wake County.
[p6.4] With a population of 283,506 in the 2020 Census, Durham is the 4th-most populous city in North Carolina, and the 74th-most populous city in the United States.(8) The city is located in the east-central part of the Piedmont region along the Eno River.
[p6.5] Durham is the core of the four-county Durham-Chapel Hill Metropolitan Area, which has a population of 649,903 as of 2020 U.S. Census.
[p6.6] The Office of Management and Budget also includes Durham as a part of the Raleigh-Durham-Cary Combined Statistical Area, commonly known as the Research Triangle, which has a population of 2,043,867 as of 2020 U.S. Census.(9) A railway depot was established in 1849 on land donated by Bartlett S. Durham, the namesake of the city.
[p6.7] Following the American Civil War, the community of Durham Station expanded rapidly, in part due to the tobacco industry.
[p6.8] The town was incorporated by act of the North Carolina General Assembly, in April 1869.
[p6.9] The establishment of Durham County was ratified by the General Assembly 12 years later, in 1881.
[p6.10] It became known as the founding place and headquarters of the American Tobacco Company.
[p6.11] Textile and electric power industries also played an important role.
[p6.12] While these industries have declined, Durham underwent revitalization and population growth(10) to become an educational, medical, and research center.(11) Durham is home to several recognized institutions of higher education, most notably Duke University and North Carolina Central University.
[p6.13] Durham is also a national leader in health-related activities, which are focused on the Duke University Hospital and many private companies.
[p6.14] Duke and its Duke University Health System are the largest employers in the city.
[p6.15] North Carolina Central University is a historically black university that is part of the University of North Carolina system.
[p6.16] Together, the two universities make Durham one of the vertices of the Research Triangle area; central to this is the Research Triangle Park(12) south of Durham, which encompasses an area of 11 square miles and is devoted to research facilities.
[p6.h3] 2.3. Major drivers for Enterprise Resource Planning
[p6.17] The City operates in an environment of largely unintegrated disparate systems, some of which are unsupported and lack reporting capabilities.
[p6.18] The City operates through a significant amount of manual interaction from users across the entire organization.
[p6.19] In addition, some strategic processes are poorly supported by the existing technologies.
[p6.20] The existing ecosystem of applications (and supporting environment of manual tools) are not sufficient to support the needed business capabilities.
[p6.21] As expectations from the business and residents increase, the absence of a single source of truth is emerging as a major challenge.
[p6.22] Multiple systems of record, along with other departments’ local solutions, create an operating environment rampant with inefficiency and manual activity that can inhibit process effectiveness.
[p6.23] An accurate picture of the operations is expected to play a pivotal role in supporting the long-term sustainability, rising community expectations, and continuous improvement of services at the City.
[p6.24] To support the City’s strategic direction, a solution must have the ability to produce efficient and timely reports to internal and external stakeholders, to integrate business processes, and to provide consistent corporate data across various users in multiple departments including the Finance Department, Budget & Management Services Department, The Customer Billing Services Division within the Department of Water Management, and Human Resources.
[p6.25] Challenges are many and include the following:
[p6.26] • Multiple systems of record resulting in the duplication of data, complicating data availability, cross checking, and reporting.
[p6.27] • Manual processes and lack of visibility into disparate systems creating inconsistent information.
[p6.28] • Time-consuming data reconciliation processes and lack of visibility into disparate systems, creating inconsistent answers to the same questions.
[p6.29] • The inability to make data-driven business decisions due to the absence of aggregate data from a single integrated, reliable set of information.
[p6.30] • Increased resource effort spent on non-value-added functions (e.g., re-keying, data validation, tracing transactions).
[p7.1] • Individual departments operating with different systems, which contribute to a lack of coordination and cohesion between the departments.
[p7.2] • A recognition that operationally, the existing tools may be a barrier to scalability and future operations.
[p7.3] With IT increasingly expected to effectively partner with the business to support organizational objectives, an integrated ERP solution is needed to address both today’s support model and tomorrow’s needs.
[p7.4] Unaddressed, the City risks not realizing the efficiencies, costs savings, and innovation opportunities needed to support the organization’s strategy and objectives today and in the future.

[p9.9] As a general rule, all submissions to the City are available to any member of the public.
[p9.10] However, if materials qualify as provided in this section, the City will take reasonable steps to keep trade secrets confidential.
[p9.11] Definitions.
[p9.12] In this section (Trade Secrets and Confidentiality) – The term “proponent” includes the proponent as contractor (that is, after it is a party to a contract with the City).
[p9.13] The term “trade secret” means business or technical information, including but not limited to, a formula, pattern, program, device, compilation of information, method, technique, or process that:
[p9.14] a. Derives independent actual or potential commercial value from not being generally known or readily ascertainable through independent development or reverse engineering by persons who can obtain economic value from its disclosure or use; and
[p9.15] b. Is the subject of efforts that are reasonable under the circumstances to maintain its secrecy.
[p9.16] The existence of a trade secret shall not be negated merely because the information comprising the trade secret has also been developed, used, or owned independently by more than one person or licensed to other persons.
[p9.17] The term “record” means all documents, papers, letters, maps, books, photographs, films, sound recordings, magnetic or other tapes, electronic data-processing records, artifacts, or other documentary material, regardless of physical form or characteristics, received by the City in connection with the proponent’s proposal.
[p9.18] (a) Designation of Confidential Records.
[p9.19] To the extent that the proponent wishes to maintain the confidentiality of trade secrets contained in materials provided to the City, the proponent shall prominently designate the material with the words “trade secrets” at the time of its initial disclosure to the City.
[p9.20] The proponent shall not designate any material provided to the City as trade secrets unless the proponent has a reasonable and good-faith belief that the material contains a trade secret.
[p9.21] When requested by the City, the proponent shall promptly disclose to the City the proponent’s reasoning for designating material as trade secrets; the proponent may need to label parts of that reasoning as trade secrets.
[p9.22] In providing materials to the City, the proponent shall make reasonable efforts to separate those designated as trade secrets from those not so designated, both to facilitate the City’s use of the materials and to minimize the opportunity for accidental disclosure.
[p9.23] For instance, if only a sentence or paragraph on a page is a trade secret, the page must be marked clearly to communicate that distinction.
[p9.24] To avoid mistake or confusion, it is generally best to have only trade secret information on a page and nothing else on that page.
[p9.25] To the extent authorized by applicable state and federal law, the City shall maintain the confidentiality of records designated “trade secrets” in accordance with this section.
[p9.26] Whenever the proponent ceases to have a good-faith belief that a particular record contains a trade secret, it shall promptly notify the City.
[p9.27] (b) Request by Public for Access to Record.
[p9.28] When any person requests the City to provide access to a record designated as a trade secret in accordance with subsection (a) above, the City may
[p9.29] (1) decline the request for access,
[p9.30] (2) notify the proponent of the request and that the City has provided, or intends to provide, the person access to the record because applicable law requires that the access be granted, or
[p10.1] (3) notify the proponent of the request and that the City intends to decline the request.
[p10.2] Before declining the request, the City may require the proponent to give further assurances so that the City can be certain that the proponent will comply with subsection (c) below.
[p10.3] (c) Defense of City.
[p10.4] If the City declines the request for access to a record designated as trade secrets in accordance with subsection (a), then, in consideration of the promises in (b) above and for considering the proponent’s proposal, the proponent agrees that it shall defend, indemnify, and save harmless Indemnitees from and against all Charges that arise in any manner from, in connection with, or out of the City’s nondisclosure of the records.
[p10.5] In providing that defense, the proponent shall, at its sole expense, defend Indemnitees with legal counsel.
[p10.6] The legal counsel shall be limited to attorneys reasonably acceptable to the City Attorney.
[p10.7] Definitions.
[p10.8] As used in this subsection (c), “Charges" means claims, judgments, costs, damages, losses, demands, liabilities, fines, penalties, settlements, expenses, attorneys’ fees, and interest.
[p10.9] Indemnitees" means the City, and officers, officials, independent contractors, agents, and employees, of the City. “Indemnitees” does not include the proponent.
[p10.10] The City may require the proponent to provide proof of the proponent’s ability to pay the amounts that may reasonably be expected to become monetary obligations of the proponent pursuant to this section.
[p10.11] If the proponent fails to provide that proof in a timely manner, the City shall not be required to keep confidential the records whose non-disclosure gives rise to the potential monetary obligation.
